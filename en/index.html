From the boardroom and the delivery front line

Five truths about why AI proofs of concept die

The model usually works in the demo. The real problem comes next: someone has to own it, integrate it into the process and convince people to actually use it.

  1. 01

    The POC has no problem owner

    The technology team delivers, but nobody in the business has the mandate, budget or personal accountability to implement it.

  2. 02

    A demo pretends to be a product

    A working prototype is only a hypothesis. Scaling begins when integration, security, process and users enter the room.

  3. 03

    We measure excitement, not change

    Applause after a presentation cannot tell you whether a solution shortened a process, reduced risk or changed human decisions.

  4. 04

    AI gets an exemption from the rules

    The experiment bypasses architecture and compliance, then discovers there is no path to production. Governance should accelerate, not obstruct.

  5. 05

    The organization is not ready to change

    Even the best model cannot implement itself. Without new roles, habits and decisions, AI remains a presentation about the future.

About me

I connect the perspectives of a technologist, advisor and enterprise leader.

I have worked with technology, data and AI for 15 years. My professional thesis is simple: AI projects in large companies do not die for lack of a model. They die between demo and production — on knowledge, integration and governance. Everything I do is about closing that gap.

During 12 years at Accenture, I built a Data & AI team from scratch and grew it beyond 70 people with dozens of deployments. I led global technology programs involving more than 350 specialists and budgets above USD 80 million. Altogether, I have delivered more than 50 technology and Data & AI projects.

Today I lead the AI transformation at Bank Pekao. Around 95% of our AI implementations are built internally, keeping the architecture, development and accountability within the bank.

I lecture on postgraduate AI transformation programs and train executives to deploy AI in regulated organizations. I also speak publicly about moving beyond the proof of concept, including in PFR’s ‘Start with Polish AI’ series.
